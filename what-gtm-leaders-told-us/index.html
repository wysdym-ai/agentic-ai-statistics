What ~100 GTM leaders told us about AI agents — and the data behind it

We've spent the last stretch in conversation with go-to-market leaders — RevOps, sales, and marketing operators at mid-market and enterprise teams — about what actually happens when you put AI agents to work. Eight themes came up again and again. Here they are, each paired with the public research that backs it up.

What we are reporting is what we heard, alongside third-party public data that corroborates it. Every statistic below is external research, attributed and linked to its source.

The eight themes

  1. "We keep adding agents. Nothing compounds."
  2. "The model was never the problem. The data underneath it was."
  3. "I can't put an ungoverned agent near my CRM."
  4. "We're paying for the same work over and over."
  5. "We gave agents access before we gave them rules."
  6. "Nobody can show me what any of it moved."
  7. "Our buyers are already buying this way."
  8. "What we actually need is a shared foundation — not another agent."
Theme 01 · Compounding

"We keep adding agents. Nothing compounds."

The agents work in isolation and demo well — but each one starts from scratch, and the stack as a whole never gets smarter.

Nearly every leader was already running multiple agents, added quarter over quarter. The frustration wasn't that any single agent failed — it's that the collective didn't improve. Work done by one agent never made the next one better.

THE DATA66.4%

66.4% of agentic AI is already multi-agent

Most teams aren't running one agent — they're running many. The compounding problem is the default condition, not the edge case.
Landbase / Market.us, 2025

THE DATA100K→97M

MCP installs went from ~100K to ~97M in 16 months

The connective plumbing exploded — and MCP was donated to the Linux Foundation with OpenAI, Google, Microsoft, and AWS backing it. But shared context didn't come with the pipes.
Anthropic, December 2025

Theme 02 · Knowledge

"The model was never the problem. The data underneath it was."

Pilots didn't die because the model reasoned badly. They died because the agent stood on stale, scattered, or ungrounded knowledge.

Leaders who had run failed pilots almost never blamed model quality. They described agents citing a competitor they'd stopped losing to, or a pain point resolved two quarters ago — failures of foundation, not intelligence.

THE DATA95%

95% of enterprise GenAI pilots return zero

MIT's diagnosis is blunt: most systems "do not retain feedback, adapt to context, or improve over time." The barrier is learning, not reasoning.
MIT Project NANDA, The GenAI Divide, 2025

THE DATA16%→54%

Grounding a model in a knowledge graph moved enterprise-question accuracy from 16% to 54%

Same model, better foundation. Structure isn't a nice-to-have — it's the accuracy mechanism.
Sequeda, Allemang & Jacob (data.world AI Lab), arXiv, 2023

THE DATA60%

60% of AI projects will be abandoned through 2026 without AI-ready data

63% of data leaders either don't have — or aren't sure they have — the right data practices for AI. The model isn't the bottleneck. The knowledge is.
Gartner, February 2025

Theme 03 · Governance

"I can't put an ungoverned agent near my CRM."

Trust is the adoption gate. A suggestion is fine; an agent writing to the system of record unsupervised is a non-starter.

The blocker leaders named most wasn't capability — it was governance. Without human-in-the-loop, an audit trail, and a way to gate consequential writes, agents stay stuck in read-only demos and never touch real pipeline.

THE DATA40%+

Over 40% of agentic AI projects will be cancelled by end of 2027

Escalating costs, unclear business value, inadequate risk controls — Gartner's reasons read like a governance checklist.
Gartner, June 2025

THE DATA40%

By 2027, 40% of enterprises will demote or decommission AI agents over governance gaps found only after production incidents

Gartner's warning is specific: treating agent governance as binary — locked down or fully trusted — is the root cause of failure. Graduated autonomy wins.
Gartner, May 2026

Theme 04 · Cost

"We're paying for the same work over and over."

Every agent re-wires the same integrations and re-pays for the same retrieval. The duplication is quiet, but it adds up.

When the same connectors and the same context get built separately into every agent, teams pay for the same lookup a dozen times. The cost isn't the model — it's the redundancy around it.

THE DATA90%+

LLM inference costs fell 90%+ in about two years

Yet duplicated retrieval across isolated agents quietly erases much of that saving — you keep re-buying the same context per agent.
Stanford HAI, AI Index, 2025

THE DATA$11B→$82B

The AI orchestration market is projected to grow from $11B to $82B through 2035

~22% CAGR. Spend is shifting from the agents themselves to the coordination layer underneath them.
Precedence Research, 2026

Theme 05 · Trust

"We gave agents access before we gave them rules."

The write access came before the trust — and now the review process is being bolted on after the fact.

Leaders further along described a strange inversion: agents got real access to real systems faster than they earned real trust. The reaction is everywhere the same — human review is being retrofitted onto stacks that were wired for autonomy first.

THE DATA1 in 4

Only one-quarter of leaders completely trust their AI systems — yet two-thirds have given AI write access to core systems

Trust is trailing autonomy. The market is voting for governed autonomy — access with rules, not access instead of them.
Kyndryl People Readiness Report, June 2026

THE DATA22%→63%

Leaders requiring human validation of agent outputs nearly tripled in a year

Human-in-the-loop went from afterthought to default in four quarters — and 91% say data security, privacy, and risk concerns now shape their AI strategy.
KPMG AI Quarterly Pulse, March 2026

Theme 06 · Attribution

"Nobody can show me what any of it moved."

There are dashboards of tokens and latency. There's no view that ties an agent's work to a deal stage.

When we asked what the agents had actually changed in pipeline, the honest answer was usually a shrug. Teams can count actions; almost none can attribute outcomes — which makes every renewal conversation about the AI budget a leap of faith.

THE DATA3 in 10

Roughly 3 in 10 agent teams aren't evaluating their agents at all

Only 52.4% run offline evals before deployment, and just 37.3% evaluate live traffic. Most agent failures aren't mysterious — they're unmeasured.
LangChain, State of Agent Engineering, December 2025

THE DATA39%

88% of organizations use AI — but only 39% see any EBIT impact from it

And most of those attribute less than 5% of EBIT to AI. The bright spot: revenue gains show up most often in marketing and sales use cases — when anyone can measure them.
McKinsey, The State of AI, November 2025

Theme 07 · Buyers

"Our buyers are already buying this way."

The pressure isn't just internal efficiency. Buyers research with AI, expect two-way conversations, and reward whoever answers first.

The sharpest urgency came from the demand side: buyers adopted agentic buying faster than most GTM teams adopted agentic selling. Leaders described being out-tooled by their own prospects — and knowing it.

THE DATA67%

67% of B2B buyers prefer a rep-free buying experience

And 45% used AI during a recent purchase. Your buyers brought agents to the table first.
Gartner, March 2026

THE DATA83%

83% of marketers say customers expect two-way conversations — but 69% can't respond promptly

And 81% would trust AI to respond to customers to help them scale. The conversation gap is now the most quantified problem in marketing.
Salesforce, State of Marketing, February 2026

THE DATA7×

Contacting a web lead within an hour makes you ~7× more likely to qualify them

The classic benchmark: 60× better than waiting 24+ hours — yet average response time was 42 hours. Speed-to-lead remains the oldest argument for always-on agents, and for grounding them so the first touch is right, not just fast.
Harvard Business Review, 2011

Theme 08 · Foundation

"What we actually need is a shared foundation — not another agent."

Don't sell me a tenth agent. Give me the layer underneath the ones I already have, so they finally work together.

The clearest signal across every conversation: leaders are past the point of wanting more agents. They want the substrate — shared memory, skills, governance, and a feedback loop — that turns a pile of agents into something that compounds. It's the "system of record → system of intelligence" shift, made real for the agent era.

THE DATA16%

Only 16% of enterprise AI deployments qualify as true agents

Menlo's bar: systems that plan, act, observe feedback, and adapt. The rest need a foundation to become that.
Menlo Ventures, December 2025
