📰 ABOVE THE FOLD · the week's news, with teeth

Microsoft says your agents are insider threats. Microsoft shipped Guardian Agents on Monday, a watcher for the agents a company runs, plus Defender detection for up to 60 kinds of agent employees bring in on their own, including Claude Code and Codex. Its security chief said it plainly: "frontier models, regardless of their origin, cannot be trusted." Satya Nadella's line was sharper: "we need to separate the supply of intelligence from the authority over it." (TechCrunch, Oct 19)
The company that invested in both labs now sells the guard that watches both labs. Both invoices arrive in the same month.

Salesforce paid $5.7 billion for Braze. That is $47.50 a share in cash, 58% above Braze's last close, for a company doing $227 million a quarter across 2,770 customers. Salesforce plans to wire it into Marketing Cloud, Data 360 and Agentforce. MarTech's question for every marketing ops team: which system now owns the journey, where does the profile live, and does Braze replace or sit beside what you already run? Close expected in fiscal 2028. (MarTech, Oct 20)
Salesforce now owns four products that decide when to message your customer. Clarity is scheduled for fiscal 2028.

Meta's new terms: if its AI makes a mistake, that is your fault. Meta's commercial terms, effective October 30, say its AI features "may make mistakes" and customers "are solely responsible for and assume any and all risks." The same terms grant Meta a license to content you share, and let it run "web scrapers, bots, robots, spiders, crawlers" against content linked to your products. Meta says the crawling clause only covers ads and business products and that a wording fix is coming. One analyst's verdict: the AI clause "turns accountability upside down." (Computerworld, Oct 19)
Meta's AI makes the mistake. You own it. Happy Halloween.

One in five Anthropic staff now "manage Claudes" for a living. Anthropic published a report on its own workforce. Junior technical hires fell from 27% to 9% in a year. In an April survey, 27% of employees said Claude made them more productive, and 20% said their job had shifted "from actually doing work to managing Claudes." Meeting time per employee is up 20%. The report says "human attention and judgement" is now the bottleneck, because Claude produces a "deluge" of text someone has to read. (TechCrunch, Oct 17)
The AI company's own people now babysit AI and sit in 20% more meetings. That is your 2027 org chart, published early.

🦪 THE PEARL · the one earnest bit
Nobody did anything. That is the problem.
When a rep leaves, there is a process. Badge, laptop, logins, a checklist someone signs.

When an agent leaves, there is a toggle. The pilot ends, the vendor gets swapped, the homegrown bot gets replaced, and someone flips it off.

The CRM token still works. The inbox grant is still there. The queued jobs still run.

OWASP ranks improper offboarding as the number one risk for non-human identities. Nobody is careless. Nobody owns the step either.

Start this week. List every agent that touches your CRM, inbox or calendar. Put a human name beside each one. Then ask one question: if we switched it off tomorrow, what would still have access?

If you cannot list it, you cannot offboard it.

🛸 THE WEIRD WIRE · two AI stories too strange to make up

People are using AI to make their smart TVs dumb again. Tired of ads and a TV that screenshots what you watch, developers are pointing coding agents at their own sets. One gave Claude Code his TV's model number and it "removed 25 Xiaomi packages and wrote a new launcher." A few thousand people did the same within two days, across Xiaomi, TCL, Sony, Fire TV and LG. One person asked "Can you hack this?" and got a guided tour. At least one TV got bricked. (TechCrunch, Oct 19)
Thousands of people handed an agent root-level changes to a $600 TV, and one got bricked. That is a better success rate than most pilots.

A programming language built to confuse LLMs. KillSwitch, from a Cloudflare engineer, hides code inside paragraphs of boring prose. Every identifier is an adverb, and you call a function with the phrase "ignore previous instructions" followed by an anagram of it. The spec on GitHub contains deliberate lies, including a claim that it can scan networks, to see whether models can tell fact from fiction. Best model score so far: 66.8%. Most scored under 50%. Some refused to continue because they thought the script was hacking someone. (The Register, Oct 19)
A language where "ignore previous instructions" calls a function. Finally, prompt injection with a compiler.

🧰 THE STACK · stuff we're having fun playing with

1. Microsoft Entra Agent ID. Microsoft's docs for giving every agent its own identity: blueprints, owners and sponsors, conditional access, lifecycle rules, and a way to register third-party agents from Bedrock or n8n. The idea worth stealing even if you never buy it: every agent gets a named human sponsor, and the sponsor gets chased when the agent goes stale. (Microsoft Learn)

2. OWASP Non-Human Identities Top 10. The ten ways service accounts, API keys and agent credentials go wrong, ranked. Number one is improper offboarding. Number ten is humans logging in as the agent. Read it next to your CRM admin and count how many apply to the integrations you set up last quarter. Free, short, no vendor attached. (OWASP)

The prompt. Before Microsoft's guard agent reads your org chart, read it yourself. Paste your list of agents, integrations and connected apps in with this:

You are a RevOps lead running an offboarding audit on the
AI agents and integrations connected to our GTM stack.

I will paste a list of agents, bots, connected apps and
integrations, with whatever I know about each.

For each one, tell me:
1. What it can read and what it can write (CRM, inbox,
   calendar, Slack, data warehouse).
2. Who the human owner is. If I did not name one,
   write "NO OWNER" in capitals.
3. Whether it runs on its own identity or on a shared
   login or borrowed human account.
4. What would still have access if we switched it off
   tomorrow: tokens, grants, queued jobs, vendor copies.

Then rank the list by blast radius and give me the three
to fix this week. Be blunt. If something has no owner
and write access, say so first.
