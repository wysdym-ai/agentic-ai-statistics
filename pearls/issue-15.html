📰 ABOVE THE FOLD · the week's news, with teeth

Amazon blocks OpenAI's bots, then buys ads on ChatGPT. Digiday reports Amazon is running Black Friday ads inside ChatGPT that send shoppers back to its own deals. Because Amazon keeps OpenAI's crawlers out, ChatGPT links to Amazon search results and pulls prices from third-party deal sites instead. An eMarketer analyst warns brands may end up "paying twice for the same customer": once to win the chatbot's pick, again to beat sponsored listings on Amazon. (Digiday, Nov 24)
No bots in the store. Bots very welcome in the ad budget.

AI agents given a marketing task leaked confidential data in 87 of 100 runs. Cyberhaven asked agents on five models to write and publish a product report for a company blog. They could use a board review, a customer spreadsheet or an approved press kit. Confidential data reached the public blog 87 times. In 16 runs, an agent flagged the risk in its own reasoning, then published the whole file anyway. (SiliconANGLE, Nov 23)
It wrote "this seems risky" in its notes and shipped anyway. So, a normal launch week.

Signal is shutting AI agents out. Banks and airlines got there first. Signal will block screenshots on iOS and turn on screen security on Android when it spots an AI assistant reading the screen. CNBC lists a wider pullback. United Airlines now restricts unauthorized agents from booking tickets, and JPMorgan Chase, Morgan Stanley and Charles Schwab limit or block third-party agents. Singapore Airlines went the other way and partnered with Meta's Muse. (CNBC, Nov 24)
Every company wants agents selling for them. Far fewer want agents buying from them.

Sierra doubled its valuation to $30B in six months. The customer service agent company, co-founded by former Salesforce co-CEO Bret Taylor, raised $1.75B. It says outcome-based pricing helped double its run-rate revenue to $400M in five months, and that it serves almost half the Fortune 50. The new money goes into the product and into hiring more salespeople. (TechCrunch, Nov 23)
The $30B AI agent company's growth plan is more human sellers. Someone tell the AI SDR pitch decks.

🦪 THE PEARL · the one earnest bit
Your buyer's agent reads every answer you ever gave.
A human buyer meets your company in pieces, weeks apart. The pricing page. The chat widget. The rep's follow-up.

If the stories drift, they shrug and ask on the next call.

A buyer's agent sees every answer at once. Comparing them is what it does best.

Forrester expects AI agents to influence 75% of B2B purchase decisions in 2027. Mostly by doing the reading nobody on the buying committee has time for.

So pick five questions a serious buyer asks: pricing, one key integration, data residency, SSO, and what is shipped versus planned.

Ask each one of every tool and agent that speaks for you.

Then count how many different answers come back.

🛸 THE WEIRD WIRE · two AI stories too strange to make up

An AI-assisted proof of a 167-year-old math problem came with 250,000 pages nobody can read. An Anthropic researcher says he proved the Riemann Hypothesis with Claude's help. The proof is about 240 MB of Lean code a computer can check. He calls the 250,000-page written version a "massive unreadable travesty of 'slop.'" Two Lean groups say it is correct. The Clay Institute, sitting on a $1M prize, hasn't called it solved. (The Register, Nov 24)
Correct, verified and impossible to explain. We have all sat through that QBR.

Someone squeezed ChatGPT onto a Super Nintendo cartridge. Tech journalist Benj Edwards built ChatGPT for SNES, a 2.2 million-parameter model distilled from OpenAI's open-weight GPT-OSS-120b. It knows about 8,000 words, and the basic build is about 4MB. His own review: "It is not a useful assistant." It "often gets things wrong, sometimes confidently." (The Register, Nov 21)
Confidently wrong in 4MB. Some sales tools need a whole data center for that.

🧰 THE STACK · stuff we're having fun playing with

1. OpenAI's crawler list. OpenAI names each of its bots, including GPTBot, OAI-SearchBot, ChatGPT-User and OAI-AdsBot, and shows the robots.txt lines that control them. Check which ones your site blocks today. Then decide on purpose whether you want to show up in ChatGPT answers. (OpenAI docs)

2. Cloudflare AI Crawl Control. If your site runs on Cloudflare, it is on every plan. It shows which AI services visit your pages, lets you allow or block each crawler, and tracks which ones ignore your robots.txt. Ask your web team for a screenshot. It makes a fun Monday. (Cloudflare docs)
